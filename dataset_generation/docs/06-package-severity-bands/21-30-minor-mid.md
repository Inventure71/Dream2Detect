# Band 21-30: Minor Mid

## Role In The Scale

This band is still `minor`, but it sits clearly above the lightest minor damage.

It should capture packages that are visibly damaged in a stronger and more obvious way than `11-20`, without yet looking substantially compromised.

## Core Definition

This band should contain packages with light but clearly established visible damage that a reviewer would not confuse with intact condition.

## Expected Defect Profile

Typical examples:

- one obvious small dent,
- one clearly bent or softened corner,
- one short but definite tear,
- two small light defects together,
- one moderate-looking stain or scuff paired with otherwise normal structure.

## Required Visual Characteristics

- overall box shape still remains mostly normal,
- no major collapse,
- no broad crushing,
- no hole,
- no large opening,
- no clearly severe material failure.

Damage may be:

- more noticeable,
- more contrastive,
- slightly larger in area,
- or slightly more numerous than in `11-20`.

## How It Differs From 11-20

Compared with `11-20`, this band should look:

- more obvious,
- less dismissible,
- less like trivial shipping wear,
- more clearly "damaged" at first glance.

## How It Differs From 31-35

Compared with `31-35`, this band should still avoid:

- clear transition toward moderate severity,
- stronger geometry loss,
- stronger multi-defect interaction,
- damage that starts to dominate the entire package impression.

## Dirt And Stain Rule

Cosmetic damage can contribute here if it is:

- clearly visible,
- non-trivial,
- still limited enough that the package does not look substantially degraded.

Cosmetic marks alone should not make the package feel structurally compromised.

## Visual Impression Test

A normal viewer should say:

- definitely somewhat damaged,
- visibly affected,
- still only minor overall.

They should not say:

- this box is substantially damaged,
- this looks moderate,
- this looks structurally bad.

## Prompt Guidance

Prompt for:

- mostly intact package with clearly visible minor damage,
- one obvious small dent or bent corner,
- one short visible tear,
- or a couple of small limited defects,
- no collapse,
- no strong crushing,
- no major opening.

Preferred prompt words:

- clearly minor
- visible but limited
- obvious small dent
- short tear
- slightly bent corner

Avoid:

- face deformation that dominates the image,
- large tears,
- crushed geometry,
- package condition that already feels substantial,
- torn-open corner,
- visible interior cavity,
- large missing section of cardboard.

## Labeling Guidance

Assign `21-30` when:

- the package is clearly damaged,
- the defect is stronger than barely minor,
- but the box still reads overall as lightly damaged rather than substantially degraded.
