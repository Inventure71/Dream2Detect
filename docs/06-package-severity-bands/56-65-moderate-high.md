# Band 56-65: Moderate High

## Role In The Scale

This is the top end of `moderate`.

It should contain packages that are close to `severe`, but still not bad enough to justify the final class.

## Core Definition

This band should contain strong moderate damage: serious, highly visible, and clearly degrading package condition, but still stopping short of major structural failure or broad severe collapse.

## Expected Defect Profile

Typical examples:

- large or deep visible denting without full collapse,
- strong corner or edge failure without complete breakdown,
- a meaningful tear or opening that is serious but not catastrophic,
- localized side compression that is strong but still controlled,
- several defects combining into a near-severe total impression.

## Required Visual Characteristics

- the package looks seriously damaged,
- geometry is clearly degraded,
- condition is obviously much worse than normal,
- defects are strong enough that the package is close to the severe threshold.

## Boundary With Severe

Keep the image in `56-65` only if:

- the package is not yet clearly in major structural failure,
- the deformation has not crossed into broad or dominant collapse,
- openings are not yet large or dominant enough,
- the total impression is "very damaged" but not "badly broken,"
- any side crush remains localized rather than consuming most of one face.

Move the image into `66-75` if:

- crushing becomes strong and central,
- geometry looks clearly broken,
- tears or openings become major,
- the package starts to feel obviously severe.

## Visual Impression Test

A normal viewer should say:

- very damaged,
- close to severe,
- seriously compromised,
- but not the most extreme level.

## Prompt Guidance

Prompt for:

- strong visible package damage,
- near-severe condition,
- degraded but still bounded geometry,
- strong denting, partial crushing, strong tear/opening, or combined severe-looking defects,
- still not fully collapsed or catastrophically broken.

Calibration note:

- this band can look close to severe,
- but it should still stop short of clearly broken geometry or a dominant major opening.

Avoid:

- mild or borderline moderate cues,
- totally broken shape,
- package-wide collapse that clearly belongs to severe,
- fully torn-open corner exposing a large cavity,
- broad side crushing that dominates the whole package face,
- unmistakably severe collapse.

## Labeling Guidance

Assign `56-65` when:

- the package is close to severe,
- but there is still a defensible reason to stop one class lower,
- usually because the structural failure is not yet dominant enough,
- and because the most serious defect still reads as localized rather than fully severe.
